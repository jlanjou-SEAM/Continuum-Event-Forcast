+ Short Range Forecast Discussion (Day ½-2½)
Short Range Forecast Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
258 AM EST Sun Nov 1 2026

Valid 12Z Sun Nov 01 2026 - 12Z Tue Nov 03 2026

...Impactful flash flooding expected in the Great Basin and Four
Corners today...

...Heavy rainfall threat along the east coast of Florida through
Monday...

...Intensifying low pressure system brings gusty winds, locally
heavy rain, and severe weather to the Great Plains...

The forecast remains on track for a low pressure system developing
off Baja California to merge with a broad upper-level trough
entering the West Coast. This merger will usher in a round of
widespread unsettled weather through much of the interior West
today, shifting east into the Great Plains on Monday. For today,
the main impacts with this system remain focused over the Southwest
and Four Corners as rainfall coverage and intensity steadily
increase this morning. Storm total rainfall upwards of 2-4" along
upslope-facing terrain in these regions could lead to numerous
instances of flash flooding today, especially for areas of
saturated soils brought by recent heavy rainfall.

By tomorrow, a rather strong and deep cyclone is forecast to spin
up rapidly over the Central High Plains. Much of the Northern
Plains will wake up to increasing winds, showers, and embedded
thunderstorms to kick off the work week. The Central and Southern
Plains can expect to see thunderstorms potentially turn severe
later on Monday with locally heavy rainfall possible. On Tuesday,
a sweeping cold front will shift the focus for unsettled weather
from the Plains into the Mid/Lower Mississippi Valley. Portions of
the Central Rockies will also have to contend with heavy snow on
the back side of this system, where a Winter Storm Warning and a
handful of Winter Weather Advisories are in effect.

Meanwhile, showers and embedded thunderstorms over the Southeast will
continue to shift toward the Florida Panhandle today. Persistent
onshore flow from a large high pressure system sliding off the East
Coast will bring heavy rainfall threat along the east coast of the
Florida Peninsula Sunday and Monday with isolated to scattered flash
flooding potential. In contrast, the same high pressure system
will provide dry weather for much of the eastern U.S. with colder
than normal conditions in New England but milder than normal to the
south.

Asherman/Kong

+ Medium Range Forecast Discussion (Day 3-7)
Extended Forecast Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
128 AM EST Sun Nov 1 2026

Valid 12Z Wed Nov 04 2026 - 12Z Sun Nov 08 2026


...A slowly progressive storm system will bring heavy
rains/thunderstorms across the eastern U.S. by mid to late week...


...Overview...

A active and relatively progressive pattern is expected to move
into eastern U.S. by mid-week, as the amplified upper trough and
associated frontal system moves towards the East Coast by late week
into the weekend. The system will support widespread thunderstorms
and showers along the frontal boundary, with locally heavy rainfall
possible across portions of the Southeast and Tennessee/Ohio
Valley. The system will progress northeast into portions of the
Mid-Atlantic and Northeast by late week. Across western U.S.
additional Pacific shortwave energy will bring an increase in
precipitation chances across the Pacific Northwest.


...Guidance/Predictability Assessment...

Models continue to show the progression of the trough moving into
eastern U.S. as well as the associated frontal system. The GFS,
ECMWF, and CMC solutions support the development of a surface
cyclogenesis over the Plains and the eastward progression of the
frontal system. However, differences remain in the amplitude and
timing of the upper trough. One of the primary forecast challenges
involves the interaction of the northern- and southern-stream
shortwave energy. Differences in the timing and phases of theses
disturbances will influence the strength and position of the
surface low, the speed of the frontal boundary, and location of the
heaviest rainfall. These uncertainties are particularly important
across the Mississippi, Tennessee, and Ohio Valleys during the
early portion of the period. Further west, models continue to show
some disagreement with timing of shortwave features as it
progresses inland, especially with the GFS/GEFS solutions.
Therefore, a multi- model blend incorporating GFS/ECMWF/CMC
solutions were used for the first portion of the period, with less
weight on GFS. To alleviate some of the differences among the
deterministic models, a heavier weight towards the ensemble means
were used for the late period.


...Weather/Hazards Highlights...

The amplified upper trough and associated surface frontal system
will progress eastward from the Mississippi Valley into the
Ohio/Tennessee Valley by Wednesday. Southerly flow will transport
Gulf moisture northward ahead of the system, supporting widespread
showers and thunderstorms along the boundary. Periods of moderate
to locally heavy rainfall are possible where deep moisture, strong
low-level convergence, and enhanced forcing overlaps. WPC
Excessive Rainfall Outlook maintain a Marginal Risk for portions
of the Ohio/Tennessee Valley and Southeast for Wednesday.

As the frontal boundary advanced eastward on Thursday, showers and
thunderstorms is expected to develop across portions of the Mid-
Atlantic, Northeast, and lower Great Lakes. There is some
uncertainty on storm development, as the strength and timing of
the secondary surface front will influence the duration and
coverage of rainfall across eastern U.S. By late week, the main
frontal boundary will progress offshore along the East Coast.
Cooler and drier condition are expected to spread into areas behind
the front, although lingering moisture and additional shortwave
energy could maintain showers across portions of the Northeast and
Mid-Atlantic into the weekend.

Across western U.S., a shortwave trough will progress eastward
across the Southwest/Four Corners and a short-lived ridge will
develop across portions of the Intermountain West and northern
Rockies. Some light precipitation may develop along the Four
Corners and central Plains, with possible high-elevation snow. As
another Pacific energy ejects into the Pacific Northwest,
precipitation chances increases. Onshore flow and additional
shortwave energy will support isolated showers mostly along the
coastal and mountain areas from mid week into the weekend.



Oudit

Hawaii Extended Forecast Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
345 AM EST Sun Nov 1 2026

Valid 00Z Mon 02 Nov 2026 - 00Z Mon 09 Nov 2026

An upper-level trough moving across the north-central Pacific will
push the tail end of a cold front, followed by drier air, through
the islands early in the period. In its wake, an elongated ridge
will become established north of the islands, setting up a
prolonged period of easterly trades lasting into the latter part
of the period. Below-normal PWs behind the front are expected to
rebound around midweek.

Models continue to indicate that a broad area of low pressure
centered well southeast of the islands early in the week may
become better organized, with a tropical depression potentially
forming as it moves west-northwestward over the Central Pacific
basin mid to late week. The degree of moisture the system helps
usher into the islands late in week will be largely dependent on
the track and amplitude the system. The GFS is currently on the
northern and more amplified side of the deterministic guidance,
but even it keeps the system well south of the islands. Refer to
NHC guidance for the latest information regarding this potential
development.

Pereira

Excessive Rainfall Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
824 PM EDT Sat Oct 31 2026
Day 1
Valid 01Z Sun Nov 01 2026 - 12Z Sun Nov 01 2026

...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FOR PORTIONS OF
CENTRAL ARIZONA...

0100 UTC Update: Adjustments to the outlook areas over the
Southwest included expanding the Slight Risk across southern AZ to
the international border. This based on the latest observational
trends, along with the more recent (18Z) HREF and REFS bump in
probabilities of >0.50"/hr rainfall rates. Otherwise pared the
Marginal Risk across northern and western NV, interior portions of
CA, as well as eastern FL. Stability trends this evening, recent
HRRR runs/trends, and the 18Z HREF/REFS QPF exceedance
probabilitieswere main drivers in trimming the Marginal Risk
areas.

Hurley

Previous discussion follows...

...Southwest...

The start of a brief, but nonetheless potent atmospheric river
event will impact much of the Southwest tonight. For much of the
day today, most areas will remain dry, or have nominal amounts of
rainfall. The real event will be tonight. A large upper level
longwave trough will dig south along the immediate west coast.
Meanwhile, a subtropical disturbance and attendant surface low will
be picked up by the trough and carried east into the Southwest.
The surface low will have plentiful tropical moisture with it,
which in turn will be hurled into the mountains. Southwesterly flow
off the very warm Gulf of California will advect additional
moisture into the Mogollon Rim region of central Arizona. This will
lead to a concentrated area of heavier rainfall in an area that has
seen numerous heavy rainfall events over the past few weeks,
leading to saturated soils. Given the favorable hydrology, and that
the heavy rain tonight will be the start of a potentially high
impact event in some areas, a Slight Risk upgrade was introduced
with this update, in coordination with PSR/Phoenix, AZ forecast
office.

The surrounding Marginal risk area was expanded to include much of
Nevada and the foothills of the Sierras as well. Heavy snow is
likely on the higher elevations of the Sierras, but just below
that, much of the river valleys that drain the Sierras will see 1-3
inches of precipitation. This could lead to isolated flash flooding
concerns. Other areas with isolated concerns include the Peninsular
and Transverse Ranges in southern California, as well as the slot
canyons of southern Utah.

...Mississippi and Western Tennessee...

In coordination with LIX/Slidell, LA and JAN/Jackson, MS forecast
offices, the Marginal Risk inherited for portions of the
Mississippi Valley were trimmed to a less than 5% risk with this
update. Soils across the Mississippi Valley remain very dry, with
less than 2% of the normal climatological moisture in the surface
layer according to NASA Sport imagery. Further, instability today
will be poor, as is common for very late October, due to extensive
cloud cover. Thus, nearly all of the rain expected today will be
stratiform, and well handled, in fact welcomed by the soils in this
area. A very isolated instance of flash flooding can't be ruled out
in poor drainage areas or where an elevated thunderstorm or two
develops over much of Mississippi and into western Tennessee, but
this is a very low-end and low confidence threat.

...East & Southeast Florida...

Another day of onshore flow ahead of a tropical wave pushing
towards Florida across the Gulf will lead to widely scattered
training showers with a few embedded storms possible. Since this is
the latest of multiple days of this kind of rainfall, the inherited
Marginal was retained with no changes.

Wegman

Excessive Rainfall Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
317 AM EST Sun Nov 1 2026
Day 1
Valid 12Z Sun Nov 01 2026 - 12Z Mon Nov 02 2026

...THERE IS A MODERATE RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FOR PORTIONS OF
THE FOUR CORNERS REGION...

...Southwest...

Few changes were needed to the ongoing risk areas across the
Southwest. An anomalously strong atmospheric river is driving a
plume of moisture and heavy rain into the Southwest from the
Pacific. PWATs remain 4-6 sigma above normal for this time of year,
with values between 3/4 inch and 1.5 inches. Further, essentially
all of the Southwest has been in a very wet period, so this highly
anomalous event is at the tail end of a long stretch of wet weather
that has left the soils saturated already in most areas. Thus, all
of today's rains will be converting to runoff, leading to numerous
instances of flash flooding. Dry washes, arroyos, slot canyons, and
burn scars will remain the most susceptible areas.

The Moderate Risk for portions of Arizona into southern Utah
remains largely unchanged as far as the area impacted, as for much
of it, the risk area is tied to the terrain. That said, the latest
guidance shows rainfall amounts have a 70-90% chance of exceeding
the 100 year ARI. This is generally the threshold for considering a
High Risk area, which remains possible. Ultimately, the rather fast
forward speed of the plume of moisture, as well as a lack of
instability will be two limiting factors which for this update will
keep these areas shy of the High Risk threshold. Nonetheless, with
the aforementioned highly anomalous moisture and strong upper level
forcing from a pair of jet streaks and a vigorous trough following
behind the rain, expect a widespread 3-5 inches of rain into
portions of the Mogollon Rim, particularly areas most perpendicular
to the southwesterly flow and in the core of the moisture plume. A
higher-end Moderate risk is in effect for these areas, mainly north
and east of Phoenix. Into Utah, the threat for flash flooding turns
to the typical slot canyons and steep, rocky terrain, as well as
numerous large burn scars that dot the southern Utah landscape.
Each of these areas could see more severe flooding, despite not
being quite in the core of the heaviest rainfall.

A second Moderate Risk is in effect for the San Juans of southwest
Colorado. Here too, due to a very high likelihood of exceeding
100-year ARIs, a higher-end Moderate Risk was introduced. For this
area, the very recent high-impact flash flooding event from last
week is top of the mind. Expect essentially a repeat, albeit with
slightly lower snow levels. This might nominally mitigate any
flooding concerns in river basins that drain the San Juans. It's
likely that spot areas will see more total rainfall here than along
the Mogollon Rim, due to steeper topography more efficiently
wringing out the abundant moisture.

...South Dakota...

A small, low-end, and low confidence Marginal Risk was shifted
north and west with this update. This will be remnant moisture from
the major flooding event occurring over the Southwest, generally
targeted at the post-midnight period tonight. Soils are around
normal for moisture levels for this time of year, and instability
will be nominal over this otherwise generally tough to flood area.
Training cells are possible, which could lead to an isolated flash
flooding risk.

...Southeast...

A westerly wave that has been traversing the Gulf will move into
the Florida Peninsula today. Abundant Gulf moisture will converge
over the Peninsula as a weak upper level trough moves over the
state, further increasing atmospheric lift. A widespread 1-3 inches
of rain is expected over much of the Peninsula. This will be the
latest in a string of days that has caused areas of heavy rainfall
over the east coast. With heavier and more widespread rain expected
today, areas where soils are already saturated from previous days
remain in a Slight Risk, which generally covers the I-95 corridor
from the Georgia line south through Miami. Some instability and an
amazing history of isolated cells producing rains of 3+ inches per
hour for 6+ hours have led to incredible rainfall totals in very
isolated spots. This may potentially continue over the lower
Mississippi Valley today. The Marginal Risk remains in place with
some small expansions.

Wegman

Day 2
Valid 12Z Mon Nov 02 2026 - 12Z Tue Nov 03 2026

...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FOR THE EAST COAST
OF FLORIDA...

...Florida...

Continued easterly flow of deep tropical moisture from the Atlantic
will allow for frictional convergence along the east coast of
Florida Monday and into Monday night. Heavier totals are expected
during the day Monday and across southeast Florida, where
instability will be a bit higher, with better moisture. The highly
urbanized I-95 corridor will also support widely scattered
instances of flash flooding where nearly stationary storms can
persist the longest. The inherited Slight Risk area was left
largely unchanged. Rain should be lighter across the west coast, so
the threat level will be much lower there.

...Midwest/Plains...

The large Marginal Risk area in place across much of the Midwest
and Plains was also left largely unchanged with this update. The
greatest flooding threat within the Marginal will be for southern
areas from Texas through Missouri, where greater instability and
terrain concerns around the Ozarks should work to raise the
flooding threat locally higher. Nonetheless the instability will
remain near the lowest thresholds supporting convection. The nature
and coverage of that convection will determine how high the
flooding threat gets. Since those parameters remain uncertain, the
Marginal remains in place. Further north across the northern Plains
to the upper Lakes, there should be very little to no instability.
However, steady rainfall will persist for far longer in this area,
resulting in more widespread higher rainfall totals, but over a
much longer period of time. Thus, the Marginal also remains in
place there with few changes. A small trim on the eastern side of
the risk area, to remove much of southeastern Wisconsin was made
due to slower forward speed expected of the rainfall shield.

Wegman

Excessive Rainfall Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
317 AM EST Sun Nov 1 2026
Day 3
Valid 12Z Tue Nov 03 2026 - 12Z Wed Nov 04 2026

...THERE IS A MARGINAL RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FOR MUCH OF THE
LOWER AND MID-MISSISSIPPI VALLEY AS WELL AS THE EAST COAST OF
FLORIDA...

...East Coast of Florida...

Lingering moisture over much of Florida as well as some daytime
heating will once again lead to periods of rain across the state on
Tuesday. Fortunately, organization, coverage, and intensity of rain
will all be lesser on Tuesday as compared with previous days. Thus,
the Marginal Risk is essentially covering the hardest hit areas of
Florida where renewed convection on Tuesday may cause renewed
isolated flash flooding.

...Lower and Mid-Mississippi Valley...

A line of storms will track east across the lower and mid-
Mississippi Valley on Tuesday. The forward speed of those storms
will be slow enough that isolated flash flooding could result,
especially in areas where cell mergers lead to longer periods of
heavy rain. Antecedent very dry soils and high FFGs should limit
any flash flooding to isolated instances, but that will need to be
reevaluated as Tuesday approaches for a possible Slight, especially
into the Ozarks due to terrain worsening any flash flooding.
Further north towards Illinois, the flash flooding threat will be
much more isolated and conditional, with the Marginal Risk
generally covering urban and low-lying flood-prone areas.

Wegman

Day 4 and Day 5
Valid 12Z Wed Nov 04 2026 - 12Z Fri Nov 06 2026

...THERE IS A MARGINAL RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FROM THE
TENNESSEE AND LOWER OH VALLEYS TO THE SOUTHERN APPALACHIANS (DAY
4)...

While differences in the details remain, the latest model runs are
showing better agreement with the evolution of an amplified upper
trough and its associated frontal boundary that are expected to
move east of the Mississippi Valley midweek. This system will
continue to produce widespread precipitation, with the threat for
areas of heavy rain and possible flash flooding continuing into
early Thursday. Deep moisture (1.50-1.75+ inches) coinciding with
strong southerly flow is expected to interact with an area of

Probabilistic Heavy Snow and Icing Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
222 AM EST Sun Nov 1 2026

Valid 12Z Sun Nov 01 2026 - 12Z Wed Nov 04 2026

...Sierra Nevada into the Intermountain West & Rockies...
Days 1-2...

An increasingly amplified mid-level pattern across the western and
central CONUS will bring widespread unsettled weather from the
Desert Southwest, through the Intermountain West, and into the High
Plains over the next few days. High elevation impactful snowfall
is likely across the higher terrain, with periods of lesser lower-
elevation snows also possible.

An impressive full latitude trough will be moving inland along the
West Coast to begin the period, and this trough will gradually
translate eastward into the Great Basin before closing off Sunday
evening. This trough will continue to push to the east through
early this week, with precipitation ending across the Rockies
Tuesday morning.

While most of the precipitation is expected to be rain, heavy
mountain snow is likely, especially above 9000 ft. The primary
mechanism for this is downstream advection of impressive IVT
exceeding 500 kg/m/s surging from the tropical Pacific and as far
east as the Central Plains. This intense moist advection, reflected
by NAEFS ensemble PW above anything previously measured for this
timeframe in the CFSR database, will further expand heavy
precipitation across a large portion of the region. Coverage and
intensity of this precipitation is expected to peak this afternoon
through early Monday morning across the Southwest, Central Great
Basin, and Rockies. However, the accompanying WAA will also keep
snow levels elevated as noted above, limiting significant
accumulations to above 9000 ft across the Sierra, Unitas, San
Juans, higher CO Rockies, Sawtooth, and Wind Rivers. However, in
these areas, snow will be impactful due to low SLR leading to
moderate to locally major (major in the San Juans) impacts from
snow load forecast via the WSSI-P.

The heaviest additional accumulations are likely in the San
Juans, Uintas, Wind River Range, and CO Elk Mts, where there is a
greater than 50% chance for at least 12 inches of snow, with
locally 2 feet storm total possible. Farther north, the higher
elevations of the Big Horns also have a 50% chance for at least 8
inches of snow. While most of the snow should remain at the highest
elevations, as the low wraps up and some cold air pulls into the
system within increasing deformation across MT/WY Sunday night
through Monday night some lower elevation mixing may occur, but
notable accumulations below 6000 ft are not currently expected.


The probability of significant ice across the CONUS is less than 10
percent.


Weiss/Snell

128 AM EST Sun Nov 1 2026

Valid 12Z Wed Nov 04 2026 - 12Z Sun Nov 08 2026


...A slowly progressive storm system will bring heavy
rains/thunderstorms across the eastern U.S. by mid to late week...


...Overview...

A active and relatively progressive pattern is expected to move
into eastern U.S. by mid-week, as the amplified upper trough and
associated frontal system moves towards the East Coast by late week
into the weekend. The system will support widespread thunderstorms
and showers along the frontal boundary, with locally heavy rainfall
possible across portions of the Southeast and Tennessee/Ohio
Valley. The system will progress northeast into portions of the
Mid-Atlantic and Northeast by late week. Across western U.S.
additional Pacific shortwave energy will bring an increase in
precipitation chances across the Pacific Northwest.


...Guidance/Predictability Assessment...

Models continue to show the progression of the trough moving into
eastern U.S. as well as the associated frontal system. The GFS,
ECMWF, and CMC solutions support the development of a surface
cyclogenesis over the Plains and the eastward progression of the
frontal system. However, differences remain in the amplitude and
timing of the upper trough. One of the primary forecast challenges
involves the interaction of the northern- and southern-stream
shortwave energy. Differences in the timing and phases of theses
disturbances will influence the strength and position of the
surface low, the speed of the frontal boundary, and location of the
heaviest rainfall. These uncertainties are particularly important
across the Mississippi, Tennessee, and Ohio Valleys during the
early portion of the period. Further west, models continue to show
some disagreement with timing of shortwave features as it
progresses inland, especially with the GFS/GEFS solutions.
Therefore, a multi- model blend incorporating GFS/ECMWF/CMC
solutions were used for the first portion of the period, with less
weight on GFS. To alleviate some of the differences among the
deterministic models, a heavier weight towards the ensemble means
were used for the late period.


...Weather/Hazards Highlights...

The amplified upper trough and associated surface frontal system
will progress eastward from the Mississippi Valley into the
Ohio/Tennessee Valley by Wednesday. Southerly flow will transport
Gulf moisture northward ahead of the system, supporting widespread
showers and thunderstorms along the boundary. Periods of moderate
to locally heavy rainfall are possible where deep moisture, strong
low-level convergence, and enhanced forcing overlaps. WPC
Excessive Rainfall Outlook maintain a Marginal Risk for portions
of the Ohio/Tennessee Valley and Southeast for Wednesday.

As the frontal boundary advanced eastward on Thursday, showers and
thunderstorms is expected to develop across portions of the Mid-
Atlantic, Northeast, and lower Great Lakes. There is some
uncertainty on storm development, as the strength and timing of
the secondary surface front will influence the duration and
coverage of rainfall across eastern U.S. By late week, the main
frontal boundary will progress offshore along the East Coast.
Cooler and drier condition are expected to spread into areas behind
the front, although lingering moisture and additional shortwave
energy could maintain showers across portions of the Northeast and
Mid-Atlantic into the weekend.

Across western U.S., a shortwave trough will progress eastward
across the Southwest/Four Corners and a short-lived ridge will
develop across portions of the Intermountain West and northern
Rockies. Some light precipitation may develop along the Four
Corners and central Plains, with possible high-elevation snow. As
another Pacific energy ejects into the Pacific Northwest,
precipitation chances increases. Onshore flow and additional
shortwave energy will support isolated showers mostly along the
coastal and mountain areas from mid week into the weekend.



Oudit

Forecaster's Toolbox (Prototype)
 
Tools Generated at WPC
These tools are NOT operationally supported. Data may not update regularly due to workstation failure and/or data unavailability.
Experimental Urban Rain Rate Dashboard (URRD)

Visualizes the probability of high rain rates that may lead to flash flooding over the next several days at over 60 major U.S. cities.

Prototype Specialized Excessive Rainfall Maps

Interface for specialized WPC Excessive Rainfall Outlook Maps for NWS County Warning Areas and States.

Local Storm Reports

Custom plots of Local Storm Reports across the Contiguous United States. Reports include rain, snow, ice, and severe weather, as well as other significant information from storm spotters.
Extreme Precipitation Monitor

Displays the climatological significance of precipitation forecast by WPC. The climatological significance is represented by Average Recurrence Intervals (ARIs) of precipitation estimates from NOAA Atlas-14 and Atlas2.
Ensemble Situational Awareness Table

An interactive situational awareness table that displays anomalies, percentiles, and return intervals from the GEFS, NAEFS, and ECMWF Ensembles (login required to view ECMWF data).

*Please note that there is currently an issue where only users on a NOAA network can access this page. We are actively working to resolve this problem.

NDFD Forecast Temperature Records

Interactive display of where temperatures could approach or exceed records within the contiguous U.S. (based on NDFD temperature forecasts)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    

NWS NDFD Max/Min Temperatures and Departure from Normal

Displays Days 1-7 NDFD maximum and minimum temperatures, along with their respective departures from climatology.

Precipitation Object Tracking

Contains 4 different tools to track precipitation objects (heavy rain and snowfall) from short-range high-resolution deterministic and ensemble guidance.

Weather in Context Prototype

Displays forecast information and its climatological context to quickly alert a forecaster when a record or neear-record breaking event is possible. This tool is available for both CONUS and Alaska.

Automated Forecast Low Clusters

Displays 0-72 hour cyclone forecast positions from global ensemble and deterministic model guidance.

Intense Rainfall and Flash Flood Reports

Displays flood and flash flood reports as well as intense rainfall observations for user-selectable time ranges and customizable geographic regions. Includes ability to download reports and associated metadata in csv format.

Experimental HeatRisk

The experimental National Weather Service (NWS) HeatRisk is a color-numeric-based index that provides a forecast of the potential level of risk for heat-related impacts to occur over a 24-hour period, with forecasts available out through 7 days.

GEFS Probabilities

Plots of GEFS probabilistic forecast of precipitation, temperature, and sea-level pressure exceeding various thresholds.
1/3/6/24-hr Changes

Change in weather parameters (temperature, dewpoint, surface pressure, etc) over the last 1/3/6/24 hours. Data is provided from the Real-Time Mesoscale Analysis (RTMA) or the Rapid Refresh (RAP).

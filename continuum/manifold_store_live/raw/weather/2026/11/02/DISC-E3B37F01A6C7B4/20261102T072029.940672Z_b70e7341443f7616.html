+ Short Range Forecast Discussion (Day ½-2½)
Short Range Forecast Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
300 PM EST Sun Nov 1 2026

Valid 00Z Mon Nov 02 2026 - 00Z Wed Nov 04 2026

...Impactful flash flooding expected in the Great Basin and Four
Corners through tonight...

...Heavy rainfall threat continues along the east coast of Florida
through Monday...

...Intensifying low pressure system brings gusty winds, locally
heavy rain, and severe weather to the Great Plains...

A widespread moderate to heavy rainfall event is currently in full
swing across the Four Corners region this Sunday afternoon as a
moisture-ladden low pressure system from the subtropical Pacific
interacts with a deepening upper-level trough moving further inland
into the southwestern U.S. WPC continues to highlight a very broad
slight risk of flash flooding area for much of the Four Corners
states through tonight, together with a moderate risk from central
Arizona to southwestern Utah, and just to the north and east of the
Four Corners. This merger will pick up its eastward motion,
tapering the heavy precipitation from west to east across the Four
Corners region through tonight. High-elevation wet snow can also
be expected especially later tonight over the northern and central
Rockies as far south as the San Juan Mountains, where a Winter
Storm Warning and a handful of Winter Weather Advisories are in
effect. In addition, the Storm Prediction Center (SPC) is
highlighting a slight risk of severe thunderstorms from the
southern Rockies to the southern High Plains through tonight into
early Monday morning.

Monday will feature a rather strong and deep cyclone spinning up
rapidly and expanding across the northern Great Plains. Much of
the Northern Plains will wake up to increasing winds, showers, and
embedded thunderstorms to kick off the work week. The Central and
Southern Plains can expect to see thunderstorms potentially turn
severe later on Monday with locally heavy rainfall possible. SPC is
highlighting portions of the Central and Southern Plains for a
slight risk of severe thunderstorms Monday and Monday night. On
Tuesday, a sweeping cold front will shift the focus for unsettled
weather from the Plains into the Mid/Lower Mississippi Valley.

Meanwhile, showers and embedded thunderstorms enhanced by sustained
onshore flow from the Atlantic combined with weak upper-level
troughing arriving from the Gulf Coast have resulted in deep
convection and locally heavy rainfall for portions of south-central
Florida Panhandle today. Continued onshore flow from a large high
pressure system sliding off the East Coast will bring additional
heavy rainfall threat for portions of southern Florida Peninsula
on Monday with isolated to scattered flash flooding potential. In
contrast, the same high pressure system will maintain dry weather
for much of the eastern U.S. with colder than normal conditions in
New England but milder than normal to the south.

Kong/Asherman

+ Medium Range Forecast Discussion (Day 3-7)
Extended Forecast Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
147 AM EST Mon Nov 2 2026

Valid 12Z Thu Nov 05 2026 - 12Z Mon Nov 09 2026



...Overview...

Unsettled weather continues for portions of eastern U.S. as the
upper trough and associated frontal system continues to progress
toward the eastern seaboard. Behind the frontal system, cooler and
drier conditions will expand from central U.S. into much of eastern
U.S. behind the front. However, lingering upper-level energy and
moisture associated with a northward lifting western Atlantic low
may maintain periods of unsettled weather near the East Coast
through the weekend. Across western U.S. additional Pacific
disturbances may bring precipitation chances into the Pacific
northwest.


...Guidance/Predictability Assessment...

The latest model guidance indicates improving agreement on the
broad evolution of the amplified eastern U.S. trough and the
eastward movement of the associated frontal system through the
first half of the period. However, uncertainty remains with the
timing and and amplitude of individual shortwaves and the potential
interaction between the western Atlantic surface low. These
difference are important across the Mid-Atantic and Northeast,
where the degree of interaction with the low could determine
whether precipitation ends relatively quickly behind the front or
lingers into the weekend. Across western U.S., the evolution of
the Pacific disturbances remains uncertain as well. Individual
model solutions differ in the timing and strength of the incoming
disturbance, but the broader signal supports continued periods of
onshore flow and precipitation across the Pacific Northwest.
Furthermore, chances for showers and thunderstorms across portions
of the southern tier will also depend on the eastward progression
of the western trough and the transport of Pacific moisture. Given
these uncertainties, a multi-model blend were used in the forecast,
with heavier weight on the ensemble means towards mid-period.

...Weather/Hazards Highlights...

The primary upper trough and associated frontal system will
continue progressing eastward from the Tennessee and Ohio Valleys
towards the Appalachians and eastern seaboard. Strong south to
southwesterly low-level flow ahead of the boundary will transport
Gulf moisture northward, supporting widespread showers and
thunderstorms across portions of the Southeast, Mid-Atlantic, and
Northeast. There is some uncertainty along the East Coast as the
primary frontal boundary pushes offshore and the western Atlantic
low moves northeastward by late week into the weekend. Depending
on the track and interaction with frontal boundaries, additional
moisture could support lingering showers across portions of coastal
Mid- Atlantic and Northeast. Furthermore, high pressure begins to
build behind the front, allowing for drier conditions to spread
across much of central and eastern U.S.

Across western U.S., additional Pacific disturbances will approach
the Pacific Northwest by late week and will maintain periods of
precipitation along the coast and over the Cascades. Some low
elevation rain may extend inland across the northern Intermountain
West/Rockies, with higher elevations favoring snow. Further south,
a southern stream troughing pattern, with additional shortwave
energy is expected to move through the Southwest/Four Corners,
which may support showers across portions along the Four Corners
Region/Rockies and central Plains.


Oudit

+ Hawaii Forecast Discussion (Day 1-7)

Hawaii Extended Forecast Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
345 AM EST Sun Nov 1 2026

Valid 00Z Mon 02 Nov 2026 - 00Z Mon 09 Nov 2026

An upper-level trough moving across the north-central Pacific will
push the tail end of a cold front, followed by drier air, through
the islands early in the period. In its wake, an elongated ridge
will become established north of the islands, setting up a
prolonged period of easterly trades lasting into the latter part
of the period. Below-normal PWs behind the front are expected to
rebound around midweek.

Models continue to indicate that a broad area of low pressure
centered well southeast of the islands early in the week may
become better organized, with a tropical depression potentially
forming as it moves west-northwestward over the Central Pacific
basin mid to late week. The degree of moisture the system helps
usher into the islands late in week will be largely dependent on
the track and amplitude the system. The GFS is currently on the
northern and more amplified side of the deterministic guidance,
but even it keeps the system well south of the islands. Refer to
NHC guidance for the latest information regarding this potential
development.

Pereira

Excessive Rainfall Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
733 PM EST Sun Nov 1 2026
Day 1
Valid 01Z Mon Nov 02 2026 - 12Z Mon Nov 02 2026

...THERE IS A MODERATE RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FOR PORTIONS OF
THE FOUR CORNERS REGION...

...Southwest...

16Z Update: No changes to the previous forecast as everything
remains on track for a high-end MDT across portions of the Mogollon
Rim and San Juan Mountains with a widespread MDT risk extending up
through the canyon areas of northwest AZ into southwestern UT.
HREF/REFS EAS signals for >2" of rainfall are both over 70% for
areas of the Mogollon Rim and San Juan range with neighborhood
probs for locally 5" or greater still running between 60-90% in
both areas. This is consistent from the previous iterations of both
hi-res ensemble suites, backing the current forecast and
expectation for widespread flash and riverine flooding capable of
locally considerable impacts across the outlined domain.

Kleebauer

..Previous Discussion..

Few changes were needed to the ongoing risk areas across the
Southwest. An anomalously strong atmospheric river is driving a
plume of moisture and heavy rain into the Southwest from the
Pacific. PWATs remain 4-6 sigma above normal for this time of year,
with values between 3/4 inch and 1.5 inches. Further, essentially
all of the Southwest has been in a very wet period, so this highly
anomalous event is at the tail end of a long stretch of wet weather
that has left the soils saturated already in most areas. Thus, all
of today's rains will be converting to runoff, leading to numerous
instances of flash flooding. Dry washes, arroyos, slot canyons, and
burn scars will remain the most susceptible areas.

The Moderate Risk for portions of Arizona into southern Utah
remains largely unchanged as far as the area impacted, as for much
of it, the risk area is tied to the terrain. That said, the latest
guidance shows rainfall amounts have a 70-90% chance of exceeding
the 100 year ARI. This is generally the threshold for considering a
High Risk area, which remains possible. Ultimately, the rather fast
forward speed of the plume of moisture, as well as a lack of
instability will be two limiting factors which for this update will
keep these areas shy of the High Risk threshold. Nonetheless, with
the aforementioned highly anomalous moisture and strong upper level
forcing from a pair of jet streaks and a vigorous trough following
behind the rain, expect a widespread 3-5 inches of rain into
portions of the Mogollon Rim, particularly areas most perpendicular
to the southwesterly flow and in the core of the moisture plume. A
higher-end Moderate risk is in effect for these areas, mainly north
and east of Phoenix. Into Utah, the threat for flash flooding turns
to the typical slot canyons and steep, rocky terrain, as well as
numerous large burn scars that dot the southern Utah landscape.
Each of these areas could see more severe flooding, despite not
being quite in the core of the heaviest rainfall.

A second Moderate Risk is in effect for the San Juans of southwest
Colorado. Here too, due to a very high likelihood of exceeding
100-year ARIs, a higher-end Moderate Risk was introduced. For this
area, the very recent high-impact flash flooding event from last
week is top of the mind. Expect essentially a repeat, albeit with
slightly lower snow levels. This might nominally mitigate any
flooding concerns in river basins that drain the San Juans. It's
likely that spot areas will see more total rainfall here than along
the Mogollon Rim, due to steeper topography more efficiently
wringing out the abundant moisture.

Wegman

...Southeast...

2230Z Update: Rainfall totals today have been quite prolific across
much of the lower half of the FL Peninsula -- with many areas
topping 2-4". Localized 5-10" amounts meanwhile were noted around
Lake Okeechobee, and most recent HRRRs continue to show additional
3-6+ inches through the evening hours as the well-developed MCV
continues to drift slowly across the area. The area remains within
an area of difluent flow aloft, with the strong upper level
divergence (broad-scale low-mid layer ascent) owing largely to a
90-100 kt upper level jet streak oriented N-S over the eastern
Gulf, placing much of the FL Peninsula in the left-exit region of
this jet streak. Nearly optimal dynamical forcing for widespread
convection, along with the favorable thermodynamic profile (ML
CAPEs 1000-2000 J/Kg and PWs 2.1-2.2"), are the key factors in this
impressive heavy rainfall event. We've expanded the Slight Risk,
matching the recent HRRR trends and aligning where the latest HREF
and REFS have the highest probabilities (>60%) of 3-5+ additional
inches of rain. We've also included a 25% area (high-end Slight
Risk) across parts of the southeast coast (including the Treasure
Coast), where the HREF/REFS probs of >5" of additional rainfall are
highest (40-70%). For further information, please refer to the
latest Mesoscale Precipitation Discussion or MPD #1546, which is
valid through 03Z.

Hurley

..Previous Discussion..

A westerly wave that has been traversing the Gulf will move into
the Florida Peninsula today. Abundant Gulf moisture will converge
over the Peninsula as a weak upper level trough moves over the
state, further increasing atmospheric lift. A widespread 1-3 inches
of rain is expected over much of the Peninsula. This will be the
latest in a string of days that has caused areas of heavy rainfall
over the east coast. With heavier and more widespread rain expected
today, areas where soils are already saturated from previous days
remain in a Slight Risk, which generally covers the I-95 corridor
from the Georgia line south through Miami. Some instability and an
amazing history of isolated cells producing rains of 3+ inches per
hour for 6+ hours have led to incredible rainfall totals in very
isolated spots. This may potentially continue over the lower
Mississippi Valley today. The Marginal Risk remains in place with
some small expansions.

Wegman

Day 2
Valid 12Z Mon Nov 02 2026 - 12Z Tue Nov 03 2026

...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FOR THE EAST COAST
OF FLORIDA...

...Florida...

20Z Update: The setup remains consistent among guidance for a
continued threat of locally heavy rainfall in-of the eastern FL
coast with the primary concerns being urban flash flooding in-of
the I-95 corridor. Latest 12z HREF/REFS neighborhood probs remain
elevated for >2" between St. Augustine down to Homestead with the
consensus for heaviest precip between West Palm down to Miami.
Overall, not much of a deviation between forecasts with the only
adjustment being a trimming of the SLGT away from the Jacksonville
area as the prob fields indicate a very low likelihood of
appreciable precipitation in the vicinity of the metro corridor.

Kleebauer

..Previous Discussion..

Continued easterly flow of deep tropical moisture from the Atlantic
will allow for frictional convergence along the east coast of
Florida Monday and into Monday night. Heavier totals are expected
during the day Monday and across southeast Florida, where
instability will be a bit higher, with better moisture. The highly
urbanized I-95 corridor will also support widely scattered
instances of flash flooding where nearly stationary storms can
persist the longest. The inherited Slight Risk area was left
largely unchanged. Rain should be lighter across the west coast, so
the threat level will be much lower there.

Wegman

...Midwest/Plains...

20Z Update: The trends continue in the trimming of the eastern
extent of the MRGL with more of WI down through the immediate
Mississippi Valley regions of IA/MO trimmed out of the risk as well
as trends have been for a slower push of precipitation into the
immediate area leading to less overall rainfall, and weaker rates.
This is correlated well with the little to no risk of >1"/hr rates
based off the latest HREF/REFS suite this morning, enough to
warrant the removal of the risk in this general area. Areas across
the Dakotas into northern MN still remain a focal point for
moderate to locally heavy rainfall with the main prospects likely
embedded heavier echoes in the vicinity of the maturing trowal on
the north to northwest side of the surface cyclone.

Further south, instability within the warm sector over the East-
Central Plains of KS/MO down through OK/TX will provide the best
opportunity for rainfall rates >1"/hr with some of the
deterministic indicating 1-2"/hr rates over the course of late-
Monday afternoon into the evening prior to the cold front approach
from the west. Models have been keying on that area of southeast KS
down through eastern OK and north TX as the main area of interest
with the neighborhood probabilities between 40-70% for >2" with
modest probs for >3" located over northeast OK into far
southeastern KS. There's a potential for a small upgrade in this
particular zone with the current look, but want to see future
trends with the CAMs as this area is on the drier side and could
very well handle local 2-3" of rainfall with the main concern over
the more urban footprints.

Kleebauer

..Previous Discussion..

The large Marginal Risk area in place across much of the Midwest
and Plains was also left largely unchanged with this update. The
greatest flooding threat within the Marginal will be for southern
areas from Texas through Missouri, where greater instability and
terrain concerns around the Ozarks should work to raise the
flooding threat locally higher. Nonetheless the instability will
remain near the lowest thresholds supporting convection. The nature
and coverage of that convection will determine how high the
flooding threat gets. Since those parameters remain uncertain, the
Marginal remains in place. Further north across the northern Plains
to the upper Lakes, there should be very little to no instability.
However, steady rainfall will persist for far longer in this area,
resulting in more widespread higher rainfall totals, but over a
much longer period of time. Thus, the Marginal also remains in
place there with few changes. A small trim on the eastern side of
the risk area, to remove much of southeastern Wisconsin was made
due to slower forward speed expected of the rainfall shield.

Wegman

Excessive Rainfall Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
733 PM EST Sun Nov 1 2026
Day 3
Valid 12Z Tue Nov 03 2026 - 12Z Wed Nov 04 2026

...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL PORTIONS OF THE
LOWER TO MID-MISSISSIPPI VALLEY...

...Lower and Mid-Mississippi Valley...

Surface low over the north-central CONUS will continue to move to
the northeast with a trailing cold front extending down through the
Plains, eventually maneuvering into the Mississippi Valley by
Tuesday morning and afternoon. Warm sector into the area of the
Lower to Mid-Mississippi Valley will be the focus of enhanced low-
level convergence with the arrival of the front leading to an
enhanced convective axis in the morning with further development
during the afternoon prior to the frontal passage. Latest ensembles
are bullish with a widespread 2-3" footprint located over the area
of southeast AR through southwest TN, down into northern MS with
the max located near the Memphis metro area. This is the most
notable area given the propensity of flash flooding into the urban
corridor, as well as an overlap of heavy rain into an area that was
already heavily impacted by heavy rain over this weekend.
Considering the setup and rainfall totals being forecast, this is
more likely to be a flash flood concern for these particular areas
with enough consensus among ensembles and ML guidance alike to add
a small SLGT risk with the centroid placed over the Memphis metro.

Kleebauer

Day 4 and Day 5

Valid 12Z Wed Nov 04 2026 - 12Z Fri Nov 06 2026

...THERE IS A MARGINAL RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FROM THE
TENNESSEE AND LOWER OH VALLEYS TO THE SOUTHERN APPALACHIANS (DAY
4)...

2000 UTC Update: No changes were made to the Days 4-5 EROs based on
the 12Z guidance/trends.

Hurley

While differences in the details remain, the latest model runs are
showing better agreement with the evolution of an amplified upper
trough and its associated frontal boundary that are expected to
move east of the Mississippi Valley midweek. This system will

Probabilistic Heavy Snow and Icing Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
211 PM EST Sun Nov 1 2026

Valid 00Z Mon Nov 02 2026 - 00Z Thu Nov 05 2026

...Sierra Nevada into the Intermountain West & Rockies...
Days 1-2...

An active period of unsettled weather begins today across the
western CONUS as anomalous moisture surges northeast from the
tropical Pacific. The primary mechanism driving this surge of
moisture is an elongated and amplifying longwave trough shifting
into the Intermountain West, with embedded shortwaves and lobes of
vorticity swinging through its base. This amplified trough, with
500mb heights below the 10th percentile of the CFSR database, will
steadily translate eastward, and combine with a modest but
favorable placed subtropical jet streak to drive large scale deep
layer ascent from the Sierra eastward through the Central Plains.

Downstream of this trough, an extreme moisture surge will push IVT
across a large breadth of the region to above the maximum of the
CFSR climatology, which will support widespread precipitation in
response to the impressive ascent overlapping this moist conveyor.
While the accompanying WAA will keep snow levels elevated, around
8000-9000
ft within the heavier precipitation corridor, some locally lower
snow levels are possible where fgen/upslope flow can drag colder
air down within steep lapse rates beneath the upper trough. Still,
the most significant snowfall is expected above 9000 ft and the
probability for an additional 12+ inches of snow is above 70% in
the San Juans. Big Horns, and highest CO Rockies, with lower
amounts expected elsewhere above 6000 ft across much of the
Intermountain West. Since this snow is likely to feature sub-climo
SLR, snow load impacts are expected in these areas.

As the trough pushes east a surface low is likely to deepen in the
lee of the Rockies and then rapidly intensify during Monday as it
lifts into the Upper Midwest/Great Lakes. This low will produce
only rain for most areas, however, depending on the exact evolution
of the associated upper low, a pivoting axis of deformation may
cross the Dakotas and into WY/CO Monday night and Tuesday. Where
precipitation falls heavily enough, some mixed rain and snow is
possible, leading to light accumulations of snow as far east as the
Black Hills of SD.

The probability of significant ice across the CONUS is less than 10
percent.

Weiss

...Overview...

Unsettled weather continues for portions of eastern U.S. as the
upper trough and associated frontal system continues to progress
toward the eastern seaboard. Behind the frontal system, cooler and
drier conditions will expand from central U.S. into much of eastern
U.S. behind the front. However, lingering upper-level energy and
moisture associated with a northward lifting western Atlantic low
may maintain periods of unsettled weather near the East Coast
through the weekend. Across western U.S. additional Pacific
disturbances may bring precipitation chances into the Pacific
northwest.


...Guidance/Predictability Assessment...

The latest model guidance indicates improving agreement on the
broad evolution of the amplified eastern U.S. trough and the
eastward movement of the associated frontal system through the
first half of the period. However, uncertainty remains with the
timing and and amplitude of individual shortwaves and the potential
interaction between the western Atlantic surface low. These
difference are important across the Mid-Atantic and Northeast,
where the degree of interaction with the low could determine
whether precipitation ends relatively quickly behind the front or
lingers into the weekend. Across western U.S., the evolution of
the Pacific disturbances remains uncertain as well. Individual
model solutions differ in the timing and strength of the incoming
disturbance, but the broader signal supports continued periods of
onshore flow and precipitation across the Pacific Northwest.
Furthermore, chances for showers and thunderstorms across portions
of the southern tier will also depend on the eastward progression
of the western trough and the transport of Pacific moisture. Given
these uncertainties, a multi-model blend were used in the forecast,
with heavier weight on the ensemble means towards mid-period.

...Weather/Hazards Highlights...

The primary upper trough and associated frontal system will
continue progressing eastward from the Tennessee and Ohio Valleys
towards the Appalachians and eastern seaboard. Strong south to
southwesterly low-level flow ahead of the boundary will transport
Gulf moisture northward, supporting widespread showers and
thunderstorms across portions of the Southeast, Mid-Atlantic, and
Northeast. There is some uncertainty along the East Coast as the
primary frontal boundary pushes offshore and the western Atlantic
low moves northeastward by late week into the weekend. Depending
on the track and interaction with frontal boundaries, additional
moisture could support lingering showers across portions of coastal
Mid- Atlantic and Northeast. Furthermore, high pressure begins to
build behind the front, allowing for drier conditions to spread
across much of central and eastern U.S.

Across western U.S., additional Pacific disturbances will approach
the Pacific Northwest by late week and will maintain periods of
precipitation along the coast and over the Cascades. Some low
elevation rain may extend inland across the northern Intermountain
West/Rockies, with higher elevations favoring snow. Further south,
a southern stream troughing pattern, with additional shortwave
energy is expected to move through the Southwest/Four Corners,
which may support showers across portions along the Four Corners
Region/Rockies and central Plains.


Oudit

Forecaster's Toolbox (Prototype)
 
Tools Generated at WPC
These tools are NOT operationally supported. Data may not update regularly due to workstation failure and/or data unavailability.
Experimental Urban Rain Rate Dashboard (URRD)

Visualizes the probability of high rain rates that may lead to flash flooding over the next several days at over 60 major U.S. cities.

Prototype Specialized Excessive Rainfall Maps

Interface for specialized WPC Excessive Rainfall Outlook Maps for NWS County Warning Areas and States.

Local Storm Reports

Custom plots of Local Storm Reports across the Contiguous United States. Reports include rain, snow, ice, and severe weather, as well as other significant information from storm spotters.
Extreme Precipitation Monitor

Displays the climatological significance of precipitation forecast by WPC. The climatological significance is represented by Average Recurrence Intervals (ARIs) of precipitation estimates from NOAA Atlas-14 and Atlas2.
Ensemble Situational Awareness Table

An interactive situational awareness table that displays anomalies, percentiles, and return intervals from the GEFS, NAEFS, and ECMWF Ensembles (login required to view ECMWF data).

*Please note that there is currently an issue where only users on a NOAA network can access this page. We are actively working to resolve this problem.

NDFD Forecast Temperature Records

Interactive display of where temperatures could approach or exceed records within the contiguous U.S. (based on NDFD temperature forecasts)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    

NWS NDFD Max/Min Temperatures and Departure from Normal

Displays Days 1-7 NDFD maximum and minimum temperatures, along with their respective departures from climatology.

Precipitation Object Tracking

Contains 4 different tools to track precipitation objects (heavy rain and snowfall) from short-range high-resolution deterministic and ensemble guidance.

Weather in Context Prototype

Displays forecast information and its climatological context to quickly alert a forecaster when a record or neear-record breaking event is possible. This tool is available for both CONUS and Alaska.

Automated Forecast Low Clusters

Displays 0-72 hour cyclone forecast positions from global ensemble and deterministic model guidance.

Intense Rainfall and Flash Flood Reports

Displays flood and flash flood reports as well as intense rainfall observations for user-selectable time ranges and customizable geographic regions. Includes ability to download reports and associated metadata in csv format.

Experimental HeatRisk

The experimental National Weather Service (NWS) HeatRisk is a color-numeric-based index that provides a forecast of the potential level of risk for heat-related impacts to occur over a 24-hour period, with forecasts available out through 7 days.

GEFS Probabilities

Plots of GEFS probabilistic forecast of precipitation, temperature, and sea-level pressure exceeding various thresholds.
1/3/6/24-hr Changes

Change in weather parameters (temperature, dewpoint, surface pressure, etc) over the last 1/3/6/24 hours. Data is provided from the Real-Time Mesoscale Analysis (RTMA) or the Rapid Refresh (RAP).

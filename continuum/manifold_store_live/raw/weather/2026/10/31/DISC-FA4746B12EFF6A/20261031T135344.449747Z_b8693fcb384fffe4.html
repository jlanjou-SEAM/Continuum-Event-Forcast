+ Short Range Forecast Discussion (Day ½-2½)
Short Range Forecast Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
400 AM EDT Sat Oct 31 2026

Valid 12Z Sat Oct 31 2026 - 12Z Mon Nov 02 2026

...Heavy rain threat renews over southern California and Desert
Southwest this evening; impactful flash flooding expected in the
Great Basin and Four Corners Sunday...

...Locally heavy rainfall shifting gradually eastward from the
southern Plains to the Gulf Coast states this weekend...

...Low pressure area brings unsettled weather to the Central U.S.
on Monday...

An increasingly wet weather pattern is in store across the
Southwest this weekend and the Gulf Coast states as a dry weather
pattern continues over much of the eastern U.S. The impetus for the
wet weather pattern in the West is a broad upper-level trough
lifting sub-tropical moisture into the Southwest, and scattered
showers and thunderstorms will persist throughout today. Meanwhile,
a low pressure wave now developing between Hawaii and Baja
California is heading toward southern California. The low pressure
wave approaching the Pacific coast will deliver a trick for the
Southwest by late Halloween night as the second wave of rain
reaches southern California. Rainfall will quickly expand north and
northeastwards across the Southwest, Great Basin, and Four
Corners. Rainfall amounts upwards of 2-4" along upslope facing
terrain in these regions could lead to numerous instances of flash
flooding tomorrow as it overlaps with widespread saturated soils.
By Monday, a lee-side cyclone is expected to spin up in the Central
High Plains ahead of the Pacific system. Much of the Heartland will
wake up to wet weather to kick off the work week with isolated
heavy rainfall possible. Portions of the Central Rockies could see
heavy snow on the back side of this system as well.

Meanwhile, another corridor of moderate rainfall across the
southern Plains will gradually shift eastward to reach the Gulf
Coast states this weekend. The great threat of heavy rainfall will
be associated with heavier showers and thunderstorms near the Gulf
Coast region. These showers and storms will continue to shift
slowly east to reach the Florida Panhandle and northward up the
Mississippi Valley by Sunday morning. Lingering thunderstorms near
the tail end of an old front will maintain isolated flash flooding
potential for South Florida into the weekend. In contrast, a high
pressure system will provide dry weather or much of the eastern
U.S. into the weekend will colder than normal conditions in New
England but milder than normal to the south.

Over the Sierra and Great Basin, snowfall will expand late
tomorrow and Sunday as the West Coast upper-low and the approaching
Pacific system interact. Over a foot of snowfall could result in
the peaks of the terrain through late Sunday.


Asherman/Kong

+ Medium Range Forecast Discussion (Day 3-7)
Extended Forecast Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
151 AM EDT Sat Oct 31 2026

Valid 12Z Tue Nov 03 2026 - 12Z Sat Nov 07 2026


...A slowly progressive storm system will bring heavy
rains/thunderstorms across the Central U.S. through midweek and
into the eastern U.S. later next week...


...Overview...

An active weather pattern continues across central and eastern U.S.
for next week, as the upper trough and associated frontal system
supports widespread showers and thunderstorms along the boundary.
Locally heavy rainfall will be possible across portions of the the
Mississippi Valley, Southeast, and Ohio/Tennessee Valley as the
system moves eastward. Gusty winds may develop across the Plains as
the frontal boundary pushes eastward on Tuesday. Meanwhile,
another Pacific disturbance approaches the West Coast toward late
next week, and will bring renewed precipitation chances to the
Pacific Northwest.


...Guidance/Predictability Assessment...

Latest model guidance generally supports the eastward progression
of a broad upper-level trough across central U.S. into eastern U.S.
through next week. Model agreement increases in regard to the
development of surface cyclogenesis across the Plains and the
eastward progression of an associated frontal boundary. Some
differences remain regarding the timing and amplitude of the upper
trough and the eventual progression of the frontal boundary through
eastern U.S. Theses differences will impact the timing of the
heaviest precipitation, as well as the extent of instability
available for thunderstorm development. Additionally, models show
agreement of another Pacific energy moving into the West, but
uncertainty remains on the some of the shortwave features and
timing of the inland progression. For the early portion of the
week, a multi-model blend incorporating the GFS/ECMWF/CMC solutions
appears appropriate, with increasing reliance on ensemble means
towards the latter portion of the period.



...Weather/Hazards Highlights...

The primary weather feature during the beginning of next week will
be the eastward progression of the amplified upper trough and
associated surface low/frontal system. Stronger southerly flow
ahead of the system will transport Gulf moisture northward,
supporting widespread showers and thunderstorms along the
frontal boundary over the Mississippi Valley and portions of the
Great Lakes and Southeast. Periods of moderate to locally heavy
rainfall will be possible, particularly where stronger forcing
overlaps with deep moisture and low-level convergence. Some
thunderstorms could produce locally excessive rainfall, especially
where repeated storms occurs. WPC continues the Marginal Risk for
Excessive Rainfall for portions of mid- to lower Mississippi Valley
and Southeast for Tuesday.

Behind the cold front, strengthening pressure gradients may
produce gusty winds across portions of the High Plains on Tuesday.
As the system progresses into eastern U.S., showers and
thunderstorms will develop across portions of the Southeast, Mid-
Atlantic, Great Lakes, and Northeast on Wednesday. As the Gulf
moisture continues to pool along the boundary, chances for heavy
rainfall increases. WPC has placed a Marginal Risk for Excessive
Rainfall for portions of the Southeast into Tennessee/Ohio Valley
and Mid-Atlantic for Wednesday. The storms will advance northeast
by late next week, leaving behind cooler and drier conditions in
the wake of the frontal system.

Across the west, additional Pacific shortwave energy will approach
the West Coast during the mid to late week, which will support
increased precipitation across the Pacific Northwest and eventually
portions of the the Intermountain West and California. The highest
precipitation probabilities will initially favor coastal and
mountain areas.


Oudit

Hawaii Extended Forecast Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
505 AM EDT Sat Oct 31 2026

Valid 00Z Sun 01 Nov 2026 - 00Z Sun 08 Nov 2026

An upper level trough moving across the north-central Pacific will
push the tail end of a cold front south across the islands late
Sunday into Monday. In its wake, an elongated ridge will become
established north of the islands, setting up a prolonged period
of easterly trades lasting into the latter part of the period. PW
anomalies will be at or below normal through much of the week.

A notable increase in moisture is possible late week, especially
over the eastern islands, as a potential tropical cyclone
approaches from the southeast. While there is a good deal of
spread regarding its track, there is a growing model consensus
showing a system developing and moving west-northwestward over
the Central Pacific basin mid to late week. Refer to NHC guidance
for the latest information regarding this potential development.

Pereira

Excessive Rainfall Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
406 AM EDT Sat Oct 31 2026
Day 1
Valid 12Z Sat Oct 31 2026 - 12Z Sun Nov 01 2026

...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FOR PORTIONS OF
CENTRAL ARIZONA...

...Southwest...

The start of a brief, but nonetheless potent atmospheric river
event will impact much of the Southwest tonight. For much of the
day today, most areas will remain dry, or have nominal amounts of
rainfall. The real event will be tonight. A large upper level
longwave trough will dig south along the immediate west coast.
Meanwhile, a subtropical disturbance and attendant surface low will
be picked up by the trough and carried east into the Southwest.
The surface low will have plentiful tropical moisture with it,
which in turn will be hurled into the mountains. Southwesterly flow
off the very warm Gulf of California will advect additional
moisture into the Mogollon Rim region of central Arizona. This will
lead to a concentrated area of heavier rainfall in an area that has
seen numerous heavy rainfall events over the past few weeks,
leading to saturated soils. Given the favorable hydrology, and that
the heavy rain tonight will be the start of a potentially high
impact event in some areas, a Slight Risk upgrade was introduced
with this update, in coordination with PSR/Phoenix, AZ forecast
office.

The surrounding Marginal risk area was expanded to include much of
Nevada and the foothills of the Sierras as well. Heavy snow is
likely on the higher elevations of the Sierras, but just below
that, much of the river valleys that drain the Sierras will see 1-3
inches of precipitation. This could lead to isolated flash flooding
concerns. Other areas with isolated concerns include the Peninsular
and Transverse Ranges in southern California, as well as the slot
canyons of southern Utah.

...Mississippi and Western Tennessee...

In coordination with LIX/Slidell, LA and JAN/Jackson, MS forecast
offices, the Marginal Risk inherited for portions of the
Mississippi Valley were trimmed to a less than 5% risk with this
update. Soils across the Mississippi Valley remain very dry, with
less than 2% of the normal climatological moisture in the surface
layer according to NASA Sport imagery. Further, instability today
will be poor, as is common for very late October, due to extensive
cloud cover. Thus, nearly all of the rain expected today will be
stratiform, and well handled, in fact welcomed by the soils in this
area. A very isolated instance of flash flooding can't be ruled out
in poor drainage areas or where an elevated thunderstorm or two
develops over much of Mississippi and into western Tennessee, but
this is a very low-end and low confidence threat.

...East & Southeast Florida...

Another day of onshore flow ahead of a tropical wave pushing
towards Florida across the Gulf will lead to widely scattered
training showers with a few embedded storms possible. Since this is
the latest of multiple days of this kind of rainfall, the inherited
Marginal was retained with no changes.

Wegman

Excessive Rainfall Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
406 AM EDT Sat Oct 31 2026
Day 2
Valid 12Z Sun Nov 01 2026 - 12Z Mon Nov 02 2026

...THERE IS A MODERATE RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FOR PORTIONS OF
THE FOUR CORNERS REGION...

...Southwest...

Two separate Moderate Risk areas were retained with this update for
portions of the Four Corners. The smaller Moderate Risk along the
San Juans of southwest Colorado was left largely unchanged with
this update. An atmospheric river of extremely anomalous moisture
will make its passage across much of the Southwest during the day
Sunday. PWAT values of 3/4 inch to 1.5 inches are anywhere from 4
to 6 sigma above normal, threatening daily records. All of this
moisture will slam first into the Mogollon Rim region, then
continue into the San Juans. The San Juans have recently seen a
high-impact flash flooding event from a similar feature about a
week ago, and soils have not had a chance to dry out due to a
series of smaller rainfall events in the meantime. Now this event
will threaten to rival the first. Thus, the Moderate Risk looks in
very good shape.

More significant changes/expansions were made across Utah with this
update. In coordination with SLC/Salt Lake City, UT forecast
office, the Moderate Risk was expanded north up I-15 to include the
several very large burn scars dotting around the I-15 and I-70
junction. These burn scars have a history of producing flash
flooding due to heavy rainfall, so the outsized impacts that are
expected with the 1-2 inches of rain forecast led to the Moderate
Risk upgrade. In addition, the surrounding Slight Risk was expanded
north to include the Salt Lake City metro area, due to urban
concerns worsening any localized flash flooding from the nearby
mountains. The heaviest rainfall remains across central Arizona and
the Mogollon Rim region. A higher-end Moderate Risk may be needed
given the impacts that have occurred from the lesser rainfall
amounts that have fallen there over the past couple days. Expect
widespread 3 inch totals north and east of Phoenix, with localized
higher amounts. All of this rainfall will convert to runoff due to
already well-saturated soils.

...Central to Northern Plains...

A small Marginal Risk area that was in place across Kansas and
eastern Nebraska was shifted northward with this update with
changing guidance. Soils are around normal for moisture amounts,
which should help to absorb much of the rainfall expected. Further,
a complete lack of instability will hold any rainfall rates down.
Thus, this Marginal is a low-end one and could be dropped entirely
with future updates.

...Southeast...

In coordination with MLB/Melbourne, FL and JAX/Jacksonville, FL
forecast offices, the inherited Slight Risk along the east coast of
FL was expanded north through the Jacksonville metro. A wave of
tropical moisture with PWATs on either side of 2 inches will cross
the Florida Peninsula on Sunday. This will lead to widespread
rainfall all across the Peninsula. The east coast of the state
continues to have saturated soils from numerous consecutive days of
rainfall. Thus, the soils will be unable to absorb much of the
heavy rains expected. This will lead to widely scattered instances
of flooding. Urban and low-lying areas will be particularly
vulnerable.

Meanwhile, across the Ohio and Tennessee Valleys, a lack of
instability and generally light rainfall over parched ground should
limit any flash flooding threat, even if a stray thunderstorm were
to form. The Marginal Risk was trimmed from these areas, and may be
able to be trimmed further to close to the Gulf Coast, where any
and all instability will reside on Sunday.

Wegman

Day 3
Valid 12Z Mon Nov 02 2026 - 12Z Tue Nov 03 2026

...THERE IS A SLIGHT RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FOR THE EAST COAST
OF FLORIDA...

...Florida...

In coordination with MLB/Melbourne, FL and JAX/Jacksonville, FL
forecast offices, a Slight Risk upgrade was introduced for the
entire I-95 corridor of Florida with this update. Heavy rain from
Day 2/Sunday night will continue through much of the day Monday
before tapering off Monday night. A tropical wave moving eastward
will finish crossing the Florida Peninsula Monday, then exit into
the Atlantic. Heavy rain that began on Day 2/Sunday will continue
into Monday. While technically there should be less rainfall after
12Z/Monday as compared to before then, the flooding impacts will
increase as the rain continues. Thus, the Slight roughly follows
where the Day 2 Slight was, showing continuity. Easterly flow will
continue frictional convergence over a portion of Florida that has
seen numerous days of rain over the past week or so. Thus, this
additional hit of rainfall is likely to continue causing widely
scattered instances of flash flooding, especially in urban areas
from Jacksonville to Miami. With the rain ending late Monday
afternoon, so too will end the flooding threat due to rainfall, so
this Slight Risk is really for the first half of the period.

...Midwest and Plains...

A sprawling mid-latitude cyclone will take shape across the Midwest
and Plains, resulting from the atmospheric river event that slammed
the Southwest. The upper level trough will still have a pair of
vigorous shortwaves associated with it, and highly anomalous
moisture to 5 sigma above normal. This will lead to a large area of
2-4 inches of rain in the comma head region of the storm from
eastern South Dakota through much of central Minnesota. Despite
that signal, guidance shows there will be bupkis for instability.
Thus, expect a long-duration stratiform rain event. Near normal
amounts of antecedent soil moisture should help to absorb much of
this rain as it comes down at a slow-enough pace for the soils to
keep up. Thus, for now, a Marginal Risk remains in place. The
storm's influence is likely to spread further east than previous
forecasts, so the Marginal was expanded to cover all of Minnesota,
Wisconsin, and the U.P. of Michigan. Meanwhile, lingering rain over
South Dakota will also keep a large area of 1-3 inches of rain over
that state, which could cause isolated flooding issues.

Where more attention will be needed in the coming days will be with
the cold frontal precipitation. Better instability, though nothing
to write home about, could support some thunderstorms capable of
locally heavy rainfall over the terrain of the Ozarks. There was
enough of a change in the location of the rainfall footprint, and
soils across the Ozarks become increasingly dry the further east
you go, both of which precluded there being enough confidence for a
Slight Risk issuance with this update, though one may be needed
with future updates and CAMs guidance resolution of the system.

Wegman

406 AM EDT Sat Oct 31 2026
Day 4 and Day 5
Valid 12Z Tue Nov 03 2026 - 12Z Thu Nov 05 2026

...THERE IS A MARGINAL RISK OF EXCESSIVE RAINFALL FROM PORTIONS OF
THE SOUTHERN PLAINS AND MID AND LOWER MISSISSIPPI VALLEY TO THE
TENNESSEE VALLEY (DAY 4) AND FROM THE TENNESSEE AND LOWER OH
VALLEYS TO THE SOUTHERN APPALACHIANS (DAY 5)...

An amplified upper trough and its associated frontal band will
provide the primary focus for heavy rain and potential flash
flooding as it moves from the Plains to the Mississippi Valley
midweek. Southerly flow ahead of the system will tap Gulf moisture,
supporting widespread showers and storms, with strong upper
forcing contributing to areas of heavy rainfall.

Model uncertainty with regards to where the heaviest rainfall
amounts are expected to occur is driven in large part by the timing
and evolution of a southern stream wave moving out into the
southern Plains early Tuesday. Some models, such as the GFS and
ECMWF, leave this feature detached from the westerlies and move it
slowly east across the mid and lower Mississippi Valley Wednesday
into Thursday; while others, such as the CMC phase it back into
the northern stream and lift it out to the north.

Regardless of how the system evolves, there remained enough model
support to maintain a Marginal Risk extending from parts of
eastern Oklahoma and Texas over to the Mid-South on Day 4.
Deepening moisture and sufficient instability supported by strong
southerly flow, as well as favorable mid-to-upper level forcing
are likely to contribute to periods of heavy rain rates. While the
possible progressive nature of the developing storms and dry soils
will likely limit the potential for widespread flooding, the
threat for at least isolated concerns could not be ruled out.

If the system does slow, as the GFS and ECMWF suggests, the threat
for flash flooding may amplify as the system continues to tap Gulf
moisture and the potential for training storms increases. While
some solutions suggest a Slight Risk may be needed at some point,
given the uncertainty, opted to introduce just a Marginal Risk on
Day 5 at this point. The highlighted area, covering much of the
Tennessee Valley and extending north into Kentucky and east into
the southern Appalachians, reflects where the ensemble guidance
indicated the greater threat for heavier amounts.

Pereira

supporting widespread showers and storms, with strong upper
forcing contributing to areas of heavy rainfall.

Model uncertainty with regards to where the heaviest rainfall
amounts are expected to occur is driven in large part by the timing
and evolution of a southern stream wave moving out into the
southern Plains early Tuesday. Some models, such as the GFS and
ECMWF, leave this feature detached from the westerlies and move it
slowly east across the mid and lower Mississippi Valley Wednesday
into Thursday; while others, such as the CMC phase it back into
the northern stream and lift it out to the north.

Regardless of how the system evolves, there remained enough model
support to maintain a Marginal Risk extending from parts of
eastern Oklahoma and Texas over to the Mid-South on Day 4.
Deepening moisture and sufficient instability supported by strong
southerly flow, as well as favorable mid-to-upper level forcing
are likely to contribute to periods of heavy rain rates. While the
possible progressive nature of the developing storms and dry soils
will likely limit the potential for widespread flooding, the
threat for at least isolated concerns could not be ruled out.

If the system does slow, as the GFS and ECMWF suggests, the threat
for flash flooding may amplify as the system continues to tap Gulf
moisture and the potential for training storms increases. While
some solutions suggest a Slight Risk may be needed at some point,
given the uncertainty, opted to introduce just a Marginal Risk on
Day 5 at this point. The highlighted area, covering much of the
Tennessee Valley and extending north into Kentucky and east into
the southern Appalachians, reflects where the ensemble guidance
indicated the greater threat for heavier amounts.

Pereira

Probabilistic Heavy Snow and Icing Discussion
NWS Weather Prediction Center College Park MD
240 AM EDT Sat Oct 31 2026

Valid 12Z Sat Oct 31 2026 - 12Z Tue Nov 03 2026

...Sierra Nevada into the Intermountain West & Rockies...
Days 1-3...

A full latitude trough with dual embedded closed mid-level lows
will haunt the western CONUS, causing increasingly unsettled
weather into early next week with rain and high elevation snowfall.

A closed mid-level low centered off the OR/WA coast will elongated
southward through Saturday, amplifying the accompanying longwave
trough along the Pacific Coast. At the same time, a secondary
shortwave lifting northeast out of the eastern tropical Pacific
will eject northeast, and these two impulses will phase over the
Southwest and then lift across the Great Basin, reaching the
Central Rockies by Monday. Downstream divergence combined with
periods of impressive PVA will drive large-scale ascent, aided by
locally impressive fgen and upslope flow.

The airmass accompanying this system will become increasingly moist
thanks to impressive IVT surging northeast downstream of this
eastward translating upper trough. The West-WRF IVT probabilities
peak above 90% for 250 kg/m/s, and although the core of this IVT
stays south across CA/AZ, it represents an impressive moisture
plume that will flood across the region beginning Saturday evening
and then persisting as it shifts eastward through Monday. As is
typical with tropical IVT, the accompanying WAA will push snow
levels to as high as 8000-9000 ft in the Great Basin/Sierra, and as
high as 11,000 ft farther east into the Central Rockies, and
although snow levels will slowly fall by early next week, the
heaviest precipitation will exist within this elevated snow levels,
and despite elevated instability and steep lapse rates, most snow
accumulation should remain above 8000-9000 ft in all terrain.
Precipitation is expected to end by Sunday night across the Sierra,
followed by Monday across the Great Basin/Northern Rockies and
Central Rockies by Monday night.

Still, impactful snow is likely across these higher elevations,
especially within the Sierra, Uintas, San Juans, and Sawtooth where
NBM probabilities for at least 12" of snow exceed 70%, and locally
as much as 2 feet of snow may fall. This snow will feature
generally lower-than-climo SLR to create snow load impacts, and the
WSSI-P indicates a 70-90% chance of at least moderate impacts in
the San Juans, 20-40% chance across the other higher elevations.
This may lead to isolated areas of tree damage in the San Juans,
where heavy snow may fall just below the tree line (around 11,000
ft). Although most of this will be above pass level, some locally
hazardous travel at the higher passes is expected as well.


The probability of significant ice across the CONUS is less than 10
percent.


Weiss/Snell

151 AM EDT Sat Oct 31 2026

Valid 12Z Tue Nov 03 2026 - 12Z Sat Nov 07 2026


...A slowly progressive storm system will bring heavy
rains/thunderstorms across the Central U.S. through midweek and
into the eastern U.S. later next week...


...Overview...

An active weather pattern continues across central and eastern U.S.
for next week, as the upper trough and associated frontal system
supports widespread showers and thunderstorms along the boundary.
Locally heavy rainfall will be possible across portions of the the
Mississippi Valley, Southeast, and Ohio/Tennessee Valley as the
system moves eastward. Gusty winds may develop across the Plains as
the frontal boundary pushes eastward on Tuesday. Meanwhile,
another Pacific disturbance approaches the West Coast toward late
next week, and will bring renewed precipitation chances to the
Pacific Northwest.


...Guidance/Predictability Assessment...

Latest model guidance generally supports the eastward progression
of a broad upper-level trough across central U.S. into eastern U.S.
through next week. Model agreement increases in regard to the
development of surface cyclogenesis across the Plains and the
eastward progression of an associated frontal boundary. Some
differences remain regarding the timing and amplitude of the upper
trough and the eventual progression of the frontal boundary through
eastern U.S. Theses differences will impact the timing of the
heaviest precipitation, as well as the extent of instability
available for thunderstorm development. Additionally, models show
agreement of another Pacific energy moving into the West, but
uncertainty remains on the some of the shortwave features and
timing of the inland progression. For the early portion of the
week, a multi-model blend incorporating the GFS/ECMWF/CMC solutions
appears appropriate, with increasing reliance on ensemble means
towards the latter portion of the period.



...Weather/Hazards Highlights...

The primary weather feature during the beginning of next week will
be the eastward progression of the amplified upper trough and
associated surface low/frontal system. Stronger southerly flow
ahead of the system will transport Gulf moisture northward,
supporting widespread showers and thunderstorms along the
frontal boundary over the Mississippi Valley and portions of the
Great Lakes and Southeast. Periods of moderate to locally heavy
rainfall will be possible, particularly where stronger forcing
overlaps with deep moisture and low-level convergence. Some
thunderstorms could produce locally excessive rainfall, especially
where repeated storms occurs. WPC continues the Marginal Risk for
Excessive Rainfall for portions of mid- to lower Mississippi Valley
and Southeast for Tuesday.

Behind the cold front, strengthening pressure gradients may
produce gusty winds across portions of the High Plains on Tuesday.
As the system progresses into eastern U.S., showers and
thunderstorms will develop across portions of the Southeast, Mid-
Atlantic, Great Lakes, and Northeast on Wednesday. As the Gulf
moisture continues to pool along the boundary, chances for heavy
rainfall increases. WPC has placed a Marginal Risk for Excessive
Rainfall for portions of the Southeast into Tennessee/Ohio Valley
and Mid-Atlantic for Wednesday. The storms will advance northeast
by late next week, leaving behind cooler and drier conditions in
the wake of the frontal system.

Across the west, additional Pacific shortwave energy will approach
the West Coast during the mid to late week, which will support
increased precipitation across the Pacific Northwest and eventually
portions of the the Intermountain West and California. The highest
precipitation probabilities will initially favor coastal and
mountain areas.


Oudit

Forecaster's Toolbox (Prototype)
 
Tools Generated at WPC
These tools are NOT operationally supported. Data may not update regularly due to workstation failure and/or data unavailability.
Experimental Urban Rain Rate Dashboard (URRD)

Visualizes the probability of high rain rates that may lead to flash flooding over the next several days at over 60 major U.S. cities.

Prototype Specialized Excessive Rainfall Maps

Interface for specialized WPC Excessive Rainfall Outlook Maps for NWS County Warning Areas and States.

Local Storm Reports

Custom plots of Local Storm Reports across the Contiguous United States. Reports include rain, snow, ice, and severe weather, as well as other significant information from storm spotters.
Extreme Precipitation Monitor

Displays the climatological significance of precipitation forecast by WPC. The climatological significance is represented by Average Recurrence Intervals (ARIs) of precipitation estimates from NOAA Atlas-14 and Atlas2.
Ensemble Situational Awareness Table

An interactive situational awareness table that displays anomalies, percentiles, and return intervals from the GEFS, NAEFS, and ECMWF Ensembles (login required to view ECMWF data).

*Please note that there is currently an issue where only users on a NOAA network can access this page. We are actively working to resolve this problem.

NDFD Forecast Temperature Records

Interactive display of where temperatures could approach or exceed records within the contiguous U.S. (based on NDFD temperature forecasts)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    

NWS NDFD Max/Min Temperatures and Departure from Normal

Displays Days 1-7 NDFD maximum and minimum temperatures, along with their respective departures from climatology.

Precipitation Object Tracking

Contains 4 different tools to track precipitation objects (heavy rain and snowfall) from short-range high-resolution deterministic and ensemble guidance.

Weather in Context Prototype

Displays forecast information and its climatological context to quickly alert a forecaster when a record or neear-record breaking event is possible. This tool is available for both CONUS and Alaska.

Automated Forecast Low Clusters

Displays 0-72 hour cyclone forecast positions from global ensemble and deterministic model guidance.

Intense Rainfall and Flash Flood Reports

Displays flood and flash flood reports as well as intense rainfall observations for user-selectable time ranges and customizable geographic regions. Includes ability to download reports and associated metadata in csv format.

Experimental HeatRisk

The experimental National Weather Service (NWS) HeatRisk is a color-numeric-based index that provides a forecast of the potential level of risk for heat-related impacts to occur over a 24-hour period, with forecasts available out through 7 days.

GEFS Probabilities

Plots of GEFS probabilistic forecast of precipitation, temperature, and sea-level pressure exceeding various thresholds.
1/3/6/24-hr Changes

Change in weather parameters (temperature, dewpoint, surface pressure, etc) over the last 1/3/6/24 hours. Data is provided from the Real-Time Mesoscale Analysis (RTMA) or the Rapid Refresh (RAP).

Prognostic Discussion for Week 3-4 Temperature and Precipitation Outlooks
NWS Climate Prediction Center College Park MD
300PM EDT Fri Oct 30 2026

Week 3-4 Forecast Discussion Valid Sat Nov 14 2026-Fri Nov 27 2026

For the current Week 3-4 Outlooks, both tropical and extratropical climate signals are considered alongside dynamical model guidance. In the tropical Pacific, ongoing El Niño conditions feature weekly relative sea surface temperature (SST) anomalies of approximately 2.5°C within the Niño 3.4 region. In the extratropics, a rare November stratospheric polar vortex disruption is unfolding, with forecasts indicating a transition into a major sudden stratospheric warming (SSW) in a week. Although this SSW will take place prior to the start of the forecast periods, its effects often persist for up to two months, typically manifesting as a negative Arctic Oscillation (AO) phase. Recent forecast guidance reflects a shift toward a negative AO. Concurrently, the Pacific-North American pattern is transitioning to a positive phase, an alignment typically associated with El Niño. A key consideration is how these various climate drivers and extratropical teleconnections will interact to shape temperature and precipitation patterns throughout the Week 3-4 period.

The large-scale atmospheric circulation throughout the 2-week forecast period closely resembles a classic El Niño teleconnection pattern. The 500-hPa height field is dominated by a wave train marked by anomalous troughing over both the North Pacific and the southeastern Contiguous United States (CONUS), contrasted by anomalous ridging spanning much of Canada, the Arctic, and the northern tier of CONUS. While dynamical guidance broadly agrees on these primary features, the principal source of uncertainty centers on the Southeast trough, where models diverge regarding the strength and location. Additionally, anomalous troughing is present over the North Atlantic. The alignment of positive height anomalies over the Arctic alongside negative height anomalies across the North Atlantic and North Pacific projects onto a negative AO—a signature consistent with an SSW response. However, the area of positive heights extends farther south into North America than typically seen in a canonical negative AO pattern.

Under the influence of ridging over much of the Great Lakes and Northern Plains, the Week 3-4 Temperature Outlook favors above-normal temperatures across the western coastline and northern tier of the CONUS. This spatial pattern of elevated probabilities aligns with typical El Niño conditions for this time of year. Conversely, increased uncertainty across dynamical and statistical tools regarding the anomalous troughing over the Southeast results in conflicting signals, leading to an Equal Chances forecast of above-, near-, or below-normal temperatures for both the Southeast and Southern Plains. For Alaska, warm SST anomalies in the Gulf of Alaska combined with prevailing southerly flow foster a tilt toward above-normal temperatures, particularly across the eastern regions of the state.

Aligning with typical El Niño signals, the Week 3-4 Precipitation Outlook indicates heightened probabilities of above-normal precipitation from the Pacific Northwest extending into the Alaska Panhandle, driven by anomalous onshore flow associated with troughing. While dynamical models reliably project this feature, any westward shift in this troughing would diminish both onshore flow and expected precipitation anomalies. In addition, jet stream adjustments linked to El Niño support enhanced wetness across the Southeast. Conversely, anomalous ridging over the Northern Plains favors a slight tilt toward below-normal precipitation.

The ongoing El Niño also impacts the Temperature and Precipitation Outlooks for Hawaii. There, above-normal temperatures and below-normal precipitation probabilities are consistently favored by dynamical model guidance.


Forecaster: Laura Ciasto
